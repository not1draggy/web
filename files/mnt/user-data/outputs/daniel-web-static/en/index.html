SaaS · Food service

HACCP system for food service

Multi-tenant food safety logging that replaced paper binders and hand-written tables.

Status
In production, v2 rewrite
Stack
Next.js 15 Supabase PostgreSQL RLS PWA TypeScript

Problem

Kitchens log HACCP records by hand. Fridge temperature twice a day, signature, into the binder. During an inspection it takes half an hour to find anything, and a missing entry gets backfilled — exactly what a compliance log must not allow.

Approach

A kiosk tablet in the kitchen. Staff sign in with a PIN and log a reading in seconds. The manager sees every location in one view and exports a period report in a click.

Why this way

Kitchen Wi-Fi is unreliable, so the app is offline-first — entries go into a local queue and sync when the connection returns. Readings are immutable: an existing record can't be overwritten, only corrected by a new one that references it. Without that single property the whole log is worthless during an inspection, so it lives in the data model, not the UI.

Problem

Sell a house yourself and you end up on a general classifieds site where your listing is indistinguishable from hundreds of others, and three agents call you within the hour.

Approach

A narrow portal for direct sales only. Posting a listing takes minutes and images are processed automatically.

Why this way

Images go through GitHub and the jsDelivr CDN instead of paid object storage. At the portal's current volume that means zero media hosting cost at full CDN speed — and when volume grows, swapping in Supabase Storage is a one-layer change, not a rewrite.

About

Most companies that reach out don't need an app. They need one specific process to stop running on paper, four spreadsheets and one person who remembers everything.

So I don't ask what technology you want. I ask what takes you longest and how often you do it. That tells us whether it's worth building at all — and sometimes the answer is no.
